# Phase 4: Forgotten Country

**Course Topic:** Political & Societal Security

**Time Period:** Day 11 of the crisis — Friday morning, 60 minutes of simulated time.

---

## Context

Last week the region ran out of things, and almost none of them came back. No mine restarted. Friday's pay never landed. The six coalfield stores that were out of staples are still waiting for an order the warehouse never received. The unemployment office paid out before it could tell real claims from stolen ones, and about a third of that money went to the fraud. Fuel and the I-81 corridor were the only things that partly held — and they held for Roanoke and Bristol first.

People in the coalfields watched it happen. They watched fuel convoys pass the hollows on the way somewhere else, and they heard officials explain why some places were served first. By Wednesday the explanation had become a sentence everyone in Wise County could finish: **the state saved the cities and left us behind.**

**The road is blocked.** Since six this morning a crowd has held **US-23 in Wise County** at the Norton interchange — the one route every truck into the coalfields uses. They say it stays closed until the state answers for last week. Food, fuel and dialysis supplies are queuing on both sides.

**The county is going its own way.** The Wise County Board of Supervisors voted last night to set its own distribution rules for anything that reaches the county, whatever Richmond says. Two neighbouring counties are watching to see whether the state lets it stand.

**There are guns at the sites.** Men who call themselves community protection have shown up armed at two distribution sites and near the blockade. Some residents feel safer. Others, and the volunteers running the sites, have been told who should be served first.

**A clip is spreading.** A video of the Governor's Liaison appears to show her saying the coalfields "can wait until the cities are running." It is not verified. It has been shared tens of thousands of times since last night, and every correction so far has reached fewer people than the clip.

**The clinics are being threatened.** Two Carilion nurses on rural visits were followed and shouted at this week, accused of "taking orders from Roanoke." Rural home visits are being cut back just as people stop sleeping.

Nobody in this phase is short of money the way they were last week. What they are short of is **being believed.** Every decision this morning will be judged by who it seems to serve.

---

## Crisis Engine Analysis

### What just changed unexpectedly?
The response itself became the story. Last week's shortages have turned into a grievance about **who the response is for**, and the grievance now acts on the response: it blocks the road the supplies need, it gives a county a reason to refuse the state, and it makes a fake clip easier to believe than a real correction.

### Which system is now stressed?
- [x] Governance: a county setting its own rules, a state that has to decide whether to let it
- [x] Information: a deepfake that confirms what people already feel, and corrections that arrive late
- [x] Infrastructure: the one road into the coalfields is the protest
- [x] Health: clinic staff threatened, home visits cut, a mental-health surge nobody is staffed for
- [ ] Economy: still broken, but it is not what people are arguing about today

### Who experiences immediate harm?
- [x] Individuals: patients whose supplies are stuck behind the blockade, drivers on both sides of it, nurses on rural roads
- [x] Vulnerable communities: households that depend on a distribution site guarded by men with guns, people with no way to hear a correction
- [x] Institutions: a county, a state office and a sheriff whose authority depends on being seen as fair
- [ ] Cross-border populations: through traffic is detouring into Kentucky and Tennessee

### What decision must be made within limited time?
**Open the road and keep order — by agreement or by force.** The road can be cleared this morning with deputies and, if the Governor agrees, the Guard; the footage of it will run all weekend. It can be opened a lane at a time by negotiation, if someone can speak for the crowd. It can be routed around, an hour longer every trip.

**Who decides, and in whose name.** The state can sign a compact with Wise County, override it, or let its rules stand. Each choice tells two other counties what they can do next.

**What to say, and who says it.** A correction from an office nobody trusts does not correct anything. The messengers the hollows believe are pastors, fire chiefs and neighbours — and they will only carry what they believe is true.

### What future risk is created by this decision?
- **Clear the road by force:** supplies move today; the grievance becomes a martyrdom, the protest moves somewhere harder to reach, and Phase 5's health warnings land on people who no longer listen to anyone official.
- **Negotiate:** the road opens slower and on terms; the next group learns that a blockade gets a meeting.
- **Let the county rule itself:** local legitimacy holds; the next county does the same, and a regional response becomes twelve local ones.
- **Either way:** whatever is decided this morning will be compared, county by county, with what was done last week.

### Who makes the decision, and who is left out?
- **Decision makers:** Acadia (National Guard), Banff (Wise County supervisors), Denali (Emergency Management), Everglades (Sheriff's Coalition), Glacier (the coalition on the road), Katmai (Governor's Liaison), Olympic (WDBJ7 News), Redwood (faith and community network), Shenandoah (ACLU), Teton (VDOT), Yellowstone (United Way), Zion (Carilion)
- **Stakeholders excluded:** the dialysis patient behind the blockade, the driver who was told to wait, the volunteer at a site with armed men, the nurse who stopped doing home visits, the families in the two neighbouring counties
- **Why this matters:** the people most affected by the blockade and the people on it are both from the coalfields. Every choice this morning picks between them in public.

## Phase Rules

- Every rule from earlier phases is in force. One report per incident during the phase, one report for the phase that evening. Name the incident by its dashboard ID.
- **An incident closes once, and reports are taken in the order they were submitted.** A report on an incident filed after it was resolved is not graded and nothing is charged for it. The dashboard shows who closed each resolved incident; turn to what it opened.
- **Budgets do not carry.** This phase allocates for the role you now hold.
- **Trust starts at 6 for every team.** New role, new counterparts.

## Learning Objectives

- See how a grievance about fairness becomes a security problem that blocks the response itself.
- Weigh de-escalation against enforcement when both carry a cost the region will remember.
- Judge who can credibly correct a falsehood, and why the answer is rarely the office being accused.
- Balance local authority against a regional response, and see what each choice teaches the next county.
