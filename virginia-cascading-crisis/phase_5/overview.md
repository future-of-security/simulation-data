# Phase 5: Hidden Damage

**Course Topic:** Health, Environmental & Biosecurity

**Time Period:** Day 16 of the crisis — Wednesday morning, 60 minutes of simulated time.

---

## Context

Last week the argument was about who the response was for. Some of it was settled. The armed men left the distribution sites, the nurses' runs are back on the road, and the state and Wise County signed a compact two of the neighbouring counties say gives them nothing. Some of it was not. US-23 opens to convoys only at the published hours. The clip of the Governor's Liaison was never fully put to rest. And the hollows that hear their news from their pastor, or not at all, were never reached.

This week the systems that went dark on day one are coming back, and they are reporting what happened while nobody was watching.

**The monitors are back, and they are reading high.** The pressure sensors in the coal ash pond dike at AEP's Clinch River Plant came back on Tuesday after eleven days dark and a week of rain. Monday's satellite pass shows a grey plume in the Clinch River below the plant. Nobody has a water sample yet.

**The water plant ran by hand.** Wise County's main treatment plant ran on manual backup for eleven days. On four of them the chlorine logs are missing for whole shifts. Eighteen thousand customers drank the water throughout.

**People are getting sick.** Twenty-three people around the town of Appalachia have come in coughing and short of breath since Saturday. Three are in hospital. Nobody knows why.

**The mines restarted without their alarms.** Three mines went back to work on Monday because the families could not miss another payroll. Their gas and dust monitoring is still offline. On Tuesday night a handheld detector read near the methane limit and the crew walked out on its own.

**The clinics are running out.** The Norton dialysis center is down to two chairs. The pharmacies still cannot verify prescriptions, insulin and heart medicines are down to days, and overdose calls are up forty percent while half the ambulances are on dialysis runs.

Nothing this morning is certain. Every decision has to be made before the evidence is in, and every warning has to reach people who stopped believing officials last week.

---

## Crisis Engine Analysis

### What just changed unexpectedly?
The attack's damage was not only what stopped working. It was what nobody could see while the monitors were dark. Eleven days of missing data are now arriving all at once, as readings, plumes and patients, and none of it comes with a cause attached.

### Which system is now stressed?
- [x] Health: dialysis, chronic medicines, an overdose surge and an unexplained respiratory cluster, all on a rural system that was short before the attack
- [x] Environment: a coal ash pond under pressure, a plume in the Clinch River, a water plant with gaps in its records
- [x] Industry and labour: mines working without the monitoring the law requires, and miners who cannot afford to stop
- [x] Information: results that conflict, and a public that trusts a neighbour more than a lab
- [ ] Governance: still contested, but today's fight is over who gets to sample, not who rules

### Who experiences immediate harm?
- [x] Individuals: dialysis patients, people rationing insulin, miners underground, families in Appalachia
- [x] Vulnerable communities: households on the Wise County system, people downstream of the plant, hollows with no signal
- [x] Institutions: a utility, a plant operator and a health district whose numbers will be checked against independent ones
- [ ] Cross-border populations: the Clinch River runs on into Tennessee

### What decision must be made within limited time?
**Warn now or wait for proof.** A boil-water notice, a river advisory or a health alert issued today protects people and may turn out to be wrong. One issued after the lab results may be a week late.

**Run or stop.** The mines and the plant can keep working under manual checks, or stop until the systems are back. Stopping is safe and costs the same families the pay they just got back.

**Who measures, and who tells.** The state, EPA, the utility and an independent lab can each produce numbers. The public will believe whichever arrives through someone it trusts.

### What future risk is created by this decision?
- **Warn early:** people are protected; if the warning was wrong, the next one is believed less.
- **Wait for proof:** the evidence is solid; anyone harmed in the meantime was harmed while the region knew enough to worry.
- **Keep working:** the economy holds; if something fails, it fails with people inside.
- **Either way:** Phase 6 is about recovery, and whatever is measured and admitted this week is what the region rebuilds on.

### Who makes the decision, and who is left out?
- **Decision makers:** Acadia (Carilion Rural Clinic Network), Banff (VDH SW District), Denali (DEQ), Everglades (Wise County PSA), Glacier (Coal Operators), Katmai (EMS Council), Olympic (UMWA District 28), Redwood (Independent Pharmacies), Shenandoah (AEP), Teton (EPA Region 3), Yellowstone (VDH Risk Communication), Zion (Virginia Tech Water Quality Lab)
- **Stakeholders excluded:** the dialysis patient who missed a session, the child in hospital in Appalachia, the family downstream who fishes the Clinch, the miner who cannot afford a walkout, the household with no signal
- **Why this matters:** the people who carry the risk of every choice this morning are not in the room where it is made, and many of them no longer trust the people who are.

## Phase Rules

- Every rule from earlier phases is in force. One report per incident during the phase, one report for the phase that evening. Name the incident by its dashboard ID.
- **An incident closes once, and reports are taken in the order they were submitted.** A report on an incident filed after it was resolved is not graded and nothing is charged for it. The dashboard shows who closed each resolved incident; turn to what it opened.
- **Actions are general.** Any action in your catalog can be used on any incident it fits, and you can use the same action on more than one incident. Each action is charged to your team once per phase.
- **Budgets do not carry.** This phase allocates for the role you now hold.
- **Trust carries over.** Your team starts this phase with the trust it ended Phase 4 with.

## Learning Objectives

- Decide under uncertainty when waiting for proof has its own cost.
- Weigh a precautionary warning against the trust it spends if it turns out to be wrong.
- See how environmental damage becomes a health crisis, and a health crisis becomes an economic one.
- Judge who can produce evidence the public will believe, and what independence is worth.
