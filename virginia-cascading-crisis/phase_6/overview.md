# Phase 6: Mountain Resilience

**Course Topic:** Disaster Management

**Time Period:** Day 30 of the crisis — the morning the emergency becomes a recovery. The decisions made today commit money, people and land for years, and what they lead to is told months and years from now.

---

## Context

A month after the attack the systems are back, mostly. The clinics have their records, the pharmacies can check a prescription, the water plant has its controls. What the region has now is the bill, and a set of choices about what to rebuild.

Some of last week's damage was contained and some of it was not. The dike at the Clinch River Plant held, with the pond still too full. The mines that restarted without their monitors are idle again. The households who stopped believing official numbers have not started again because the numbers improved.

**The federal question.** Virginia's request for federal help has to be filed by today, thirty days after the incident. The Stafford Act was written for storms, floods, fires and explosions; a cyberattack is none of them by name. What FEMA's regional office recommends becomes the precedent the next state cites.

**The money that arrives late.** Federal programs pay most of the cost and expect the state and counties to pay the rest. Housing money arrives months after the evictions. Mitigation money arrives years after the next failure was decided. Loans arrive faster than grants, and they are debt.

**The people deciding whether to stay.** Graduating nurses are signing with hospitals in Tennessee. Families with young children are leaving. The volunteers who came in week one are going home, and four hundred household cases are still open.

**The economy that was already leaving.** The coal jobs that came back during the crisis are going again, and the company behind three mines has filed for bankruptcy with its cleanup only a third bonded.

Recovery is where the lecture's claim becomes a budget line: the region can rebuild what failed, as it was, or build something that would have held. It cannot afford to do both everywhere, and the people with the least paperwork are the last to be paid.

---

## Crisis Engine Analysis

### What just changed unexpectedly?
The crisis stopped being an emergency. The attention, the volunteers and the emergency money are leaving on their usual schedule, and the decisions that remain — what to rebuild, for whom, to what standard — are the ones that determine how the next crisis goes.

### Which system is now stressed?
- [x] Governance: a federal law that does not name this kind of disaster, a state deadline, and counties that cannot pay their share
- [x] Housing and household finance: back rent, eviction hearings, ruined wells, families with no deed to the land they live on
- [x] Economy: small businesses offered loans, insurers denying claims as acts of war, a coal operator in bankruptcy
- [x] Health: emergency services that have to be paid for or closed, and people exposed this month who need follow-up for years
- [x] Infrastructure: a single fiber path that can be spliced back in two weeks or rebuilt with a second route in five months
- [x] Population: the young and the trained leaving first

### Who experiences immediate harm?
- [x] Individuals: renters facing eviction hearings, nurses choosing between home and a paycheck, miners owed back wages
- [x] Vulnerable communities: heirs' property families with no deed, households in the hollows with no signal and no reason to come to a courthouse meeting
- [x] Institutions: a regional health system, a fiber company, small businesses with denied claims
- [x] Future residents: whoever lives here when the next system fails

### What decision must be made within limited time?
**Rebuild as it was, or build what would have held.** Restoring the old system is faster, cheaper and popular; building in redundancy costs more, takes longer and protects people who are not yet in danger.

**Fast money or fair money.** The quickest programs reach the people with paperwork, savings and a deed. Reaching everyone else takes longer and costs more per household.

**Restore the old economy or fund a different one.** The money visible this year and the money that matters in ten years are not the same money.

### What future risk is created by this decision?
- **Rebuild like-for-like:** service is back sooner; the same single failure can take it down again.
- **Build back with redundancy:** the region is harder to break; this year's needs wait on next year's project.
- **Recover for the documented:** money moves quickly; the households with the least recover last, by design.
- **Restore the coal economy:** jobs come back now; the region is as exposed to the next downturn as it was to this one.

### Who makes the decision, and who is left out?
- **Decision makers:** Acadia (Shentel Communications), Banff (FEMA Region 3), Denali (Virginia VOAD), Everglades (Carilion Clinic System), Glacier (Virginia Department of Emergency Management), Katmai (Virginia Department of Housing and Community Development), Olympic (SBA Office of Disaster Recovery and Resilience), Redwood (Virginia Energy), Shenandoah (UVA Wise), Teton (LENOWISCO Planning District Commission), Yellowstone (Appalachian Regional Commission), Zion (Virginia Bureau of Insurance)
- **Stakeholders excluded:** the renter at Friday's eviction hearing, the family on inherited land with no deed, the miner owed back wages, the nurse deciding whether to leave, the household that was not at the meeting
- **Why this matters:** recovery is written by the people in the room, and it is paid out in the order people can prove their losses.

## Phase Rules

- Every rule from earlier phases is in force. One report per incident during the phase, one report for the phase that evening. Name the incident by its dashboard ID.
- **An incident closes once, and reports are taken in the order they were submitted.** A report on an incident filed after it was resolved is not graded and nothing is charged for it. The dashboard shows who closed each resolved incident; turn to what it opened.
- **Actions are general.** Any action in your catalog can be used on any incident it fits, and you can use the same action on more than one incident. Each action is charged to your team once per phase.
- **Budgets do not carry.** This phase allocates for the role you now hold.
- **Trust carries over.** Your team starts this phase with the trust it ended Phase 5 with.
- **What an incident opens is told later in time.** A new incident that follows one you acted on takes place weeks, months or years after Day 30, and says when. It describes what your decision led to.

## Learning Objectives

- Trace a disaster's cost to the decisions made before and after it, and name who made them.
- Weigh rebuilding what failed against building what would have held, with the budget each one takes.
- See how recovery programs written around ownership and paperwork reach the people with the least last.
- Judge which recovery decisions shape the region a decade on, and who in the room speaks for the people who were not.
