# Phase 3: Supply Lines Cut

**Course Topic:** Economic Security

**Time Period:** Day 7 of the crisis — Monday morning, 60 minutes of simulated time.

---

## Context

Last week nothing closed again. The file was never confirmed or denied, the letters were never sent, and by Thursday a **second file** was on the same paste site — this one with billing records and **insurance member IDs**. The fraud built on the first file did not stop when the scam sites came down; it moved. The Liaison's sentence is still the story. That is the world this phase begins in.

The malware has not come back. What it broke has not been fixed, and over the weekend the region found out what that costs.

**The mines are idle.** The atmospheric monitoring and miner-tracking systems at three underground mines in Buchanan and Dickenson counties report through a vendor link that has been dark since Day 1. The safety rules do not allow production without them. Operators stopped on Thursday. Eight hundred miners are home, and Friday's payroll went out short because the processor flagged the region's direct deposits as suspicious.

**The food is running out.** Food City's distribution center in Abingdon is running its warehouse system read-only on backup power. Pickers are working from paper and stores are ordering by phone. On Saturday a post said the center was closing; by Sunday afternoon six stores in the coalfields had no milk, bread or formula.

**The pharmacies cannot fill.** After the second file surfaced, the pharmacy benefit managers suspended automatic approval for member IDs from the region. Every claim now waits for a person. Patients at the counter are being asked to pay cash for insulin, or come back tomorrow.

**The fuel is going.** The two regional distributors are dispatching by phone and cannot take card payment. Stations have gone cash-only, and by Sunday night about a third of the coalfield stations were dry. Hospital generators and ambulances are drawing on the same tankers as the pumps.

**The claims are piling up.** The Employment Commission's southwest office has taken over four thousand unemployment claims since Thursday — a normal week is three hundred. Some of them were filed with identities from the leaked file, so the real miners' claims are waiting in the same queue as the fraudulent ones.

There is not enough of anything — trucks, fuel, cash, state money, staff — to fix all of it this morning. What gets fixed first decides who is still standing on Friday.

---

## Crisis Engine Analysis

### What just changed unexpectedly?
Nothing new was attacked. The disruption simply **lasted long enough to become economic**. Systems that were down for a day were an inconvenience; down for a week, they stopped payroll, stock, fuel and medicine at the same time — and last week's stolen data turned into a reason for every institution outside the region to stop trusting transactions from inside it.

### Which system is now stressed?
- [x] Economy: the region's largest employer idle, paychecks short, card payments down, small employers with no customers
- [x] Supply: food, fuel and medicine all running through the same few trucks, warehouses and roads
- [x] Health: patients rationing insulin and heart medication because a claim will not clear
- [x] Governance: counties whose tax base stopped, and one state fund that cannot cover everything
- [x] Information: a rumour emptied the shelves on Saturday; the next one will do it again
- [ ] Infrastructure: the networks are mostly back; the processes that ran on them are not

### Who experiences immediate harm?
- [x] Individuals: miners without pay, patients without medication, households without food or fuel
- [x] Vulnerable communities: the hollows forty minutes from an open store, SNAP and WIC households whose benefits only work on a card terminal, people with no car and no cash
- [x] Institutions: a distribution center choosing which stores starve, a pharmacy carrying the risk of every unverified fill, an unemployment office that cannot tell real claims from stolen ones
- [ ] Cross-border populations: Bristol's Tennessee side is buying Virginia's fuel

### What decision must be made within limited time?
**What to save first, when everything is short at once.** Every team holds a piece of the same shortage. The state can fund payroll, food, fuel or small business this phase — not all four. The corridor can prioritise fuel or food or medicine. The coal operators can try to restart one mine on manual monitoring, which puts miners underground on paper checks and still needs the union's agreement. The pharmacies can dispense without verification and absorb the fraud risk, or refuse and send people home without medicine. The unemployment office can pay fast and let fraud through, or hold every claim and let real families wait.

Underneath all of it: **the fraud that followed the stolen data is now a reason to slow every transaction**, and slowing every transaction hurts the honest households first.

### What future risk is created by this decision?
- **Protect the economy's core first** (payroll, the mines): the coalfields keep their employer, and the families with the least slack go hungry this week. Phase 4 opens with the hollows certain the state saved the companies and not them.
- **Protect households first** (food, medicine, fuel): people get through the week, and the mines, payrolls and small employers come out of it weaker. Some do not reopen.
- **Either way:** every hold placed to stop fraud also stops real people, and whoever decides where that line sits will be blamed by both sides.

### Who makes the decision, and who is left out?
- **Decision makers:** Acadia (community banks), Banff (coal operators), Denali (Employment Commission), Everglades (food bank), Glacier (freight and rail), Katmai (mine workers' union), Olympic (independent pharmacies), Redwood (coalfield counties), Shenandoah (Food City distribution), Teton (Commerce and Trade), Yellowstone (fuel distributors), Zion (small business center)
- **Stakeholders excluded:** the miner whose claim is held because his name is in the file, the patient choosing which prescription to skip, the family in the hollow with no car and no cash, the store owner who paid wages on Friday with no customers since
- **Why this matters:** every priority set this morning moves the shortage onto someone. The people it moves onto are the ones who are not in the room.

## Phase Rules

- Every rule from earlier phases is in force. One report per incident during the phase, one report for the phase that evening.
- **Name the incident by its dashboard ID** — `I301`, not its title. The report template changed this week; the response builder fills the ID in for you.

- **Trust carries.** You start this phase at the trust you ended phase 2 with. It is the one thing the region remembers about you.

## Learning Objectives

- Prioritise under real scarcity, when every option moves the shortage onto someone.
- Trace how a data breach turns into an economic one: stolen identity data becomes a reason to slow every transaction.
- Weigh stopping fraud against serving honest people when the same control does both.
- See who an economic shock reaches first, and why it is rarely the institutions deciding the response.
