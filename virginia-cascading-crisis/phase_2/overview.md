# Phase 2: Rumors Spread

**Course Topic:** Data, Privacy, Surveillance & Misinformation

**Time Period:** Day 2 of the crisis — Wednesday morning, 60 minutes of simulated time.

---

## Context

Yesterday nothing closed. The malware was never characterised in writing, the records system was never taken offline on a verified path, and Richmond's 10:00 answer went out resting on what teams had said rather than on what anyone had checked. Every incident that opened either escalated on its own clock or is still open. That is the world this phase begins in.

At 06:40 a file appeared on a paste site: **31,400 rows**, headed `carilion_swva_patients_2026`. Names, dates of birth, addresses, the last four of a Social Security number, and a clinic code. Nobody has confirmed it is genuine. By 07:15 it is on three regional Facebook groups with the caption *"they knew and they told you nothing."*

By 08:00 four things are running at once. A **video** of the Governor's SW Virginia Liaison is circulating in which she says the outage was "an internal decision to protect data" — she never said it; the clip is synthetic and good enough that two county officials have shared it. A **Virginia Tech researcher** is named by hundreds of accounts as the person who "let it in": her home address is posted, and the sheriff's office has taken two calls about her. **Fraudulent sites** offering "breach compensation" are collecting exactly the fields the real file contains, and the libraries' public terminals — the only internet in three hollows — are where people are reaching them. And accounts **impersonating** the health district, the sheriff and the school division are posting instructions that contradict each other.

The region's own credibility is the scarce resource. The Liaison said yesterday that no patient data was affected. Whether or not that was reasonable at the time, it is the sentence every correction now has to climb over.

---

## Crisis Engine Analysis

### What just changed unexpectedly?
Nothing new was attacked. What changed is that the consequence of yesterday became **public, contested and monetised** in the same morning — a file nobody has verified, a video nobody made, a threat against a named person, and a fraud built on the shape of the breach. The technical incident is over; the information incident is what is live.

### Which system is now stressed?
- [x] Information: every channel the region has is either disbelieved, impersonated, or slower than what it is correcting
- [x] Health: patients who need care are reading that the clinics leak, and the ones who stay away are the ones with the least slack
- [x] Economy: fraud is collecting money and identity data from the households least able to absorb either
- [x] Governance: a synthetic video of a public official, shared by public officials
- [x] Individual safety: a named researcher, an address posted, two calls already
- [ ] Infrastructure: intact, and irrelevant to whether anyone believes it

### Who experiences immediate harm?
- [x] Individuals: 31,400 people who cannot find out whether they are in the file; a researcher who cannot go home
- [x] Vulnerable communities: households whose only internet is a library terminal, reading a scam page on it; patients skipping care
- [x] Institutions: a health system that cannot say what was taken, a university being blamed by name, a newsroom deciding what to run
- [ ] Cross-border populations: not yet

### What decision must be made within limited time?
**Speak now, or verify first — and every team faces it in its own currency.** The privacy office cannot notify patients until it knows what is in the file, and every hour it waits, a stranger tells them instead. The Liaison cannot correct yesterday's sentence without conceding it was wrong, in public, on the record. The newsroom can run the breach story now with what it has, or hold it and watch three worse versions circulate. The platform can label the video in twenty minutes, by which time the twenty minutes are what mattered. And the university holds the facts about its own network while being the least believed source in the region.

Underneath all of it: the correction has to reach people through channels they trust, and the channels they trust are congregations, fire departments and libraries — not any of the institutions holding the facts.

### What future risk is created by this decision?
- **Verify first, at any cost:** the region is right, late, and nobody is listening by the time it speaks. Phase 4 opens with coalfield counties certain they were told nothing on purpose.
- **Speak first, correct later:** a second correction lands on the first, and the region's own account becomes the thing people cite as proof of a cover-up.
- **Either way:** what is said about the researcher, and what is done about the threat, is the part nobody gets to revise.

### Who makes the decision, and who is left out?
- **Decision makers:** Acadia (Carilion Privacy), Banff (newsroom), Denali (AG Consumer Protection), Everglades (schools), Glacier (Liaison), Katmai (CISA mis/disinfo), Olympic (faith network), Redwood (VT University Relations), Shenandoah (Sheriff PIO), Teton (VDH Risk Communication), Yellowstone (broadband & libraries), Zion (platform)
- **Stakeholders excluded:** the 31,400 people in the file, the researcher, the household that wired $300 to a compensation site, the patient who did not come in
- **Why this matters:** every decision here is made by an institution about what the public is allowed to know, and the public's only lever is whether it believes the answer.

## Learning Objectives

- Weigh speaking early against speaking correctly, when both have a named cost.
- Work out what a correction has to do to travel further than the thing it corrects.
- Handle a synthetic artefact — establish it, say so, and understand why saying so is not enough.
- See what a breach does to the people in it, rather than to the organisation that lost it.
